The Historical Journey of Physical Therapy Education (1956 – Present)

The evolution from early technical diplomas to a clinical doctorate degree spans over six decades in Pakistan.

Physical therapy education in Pakistan has undergone structural advancements to transition from secondary clinical support to autonomous medical rehabilitation.

Key Historical Milestones

  1. 1956 – The Inception (JPMC Karachi): The Ministry of Health, in partnership with the World Health Organization (WHO), established Pakistan's first School of Physical Therapy at Jinnah Postgraduate Medical Centre (JPMC), Karachi, offering a 2-year diploma.
  2. 1963 – Transition to Bachelor's Degree: The program was upgraded to a 3-year BSc in Physiotherapy affiliated with the University of Karachi.
  3. 1986 – Expansion to Punjab: King Edward Medical College established the second major physiotherapy school in Lahore.
  4. 1999 – Upgrade to 4-Year BS: Recognizing increasing global educational standards, Pakistani universities upgraded the course to a 4-year BS degree.
  5. 2008 – The Birth of 5-Year DPT: Riphah International University launched the 5-year Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) program, aligning Pakistan's curriculum with the US Vision 2020 standards.

Historical Impact

This 50-year evolution upgraded physical therapists from technical assistants working strictly under prescription to autonomous primary care clinicians capable of direct patient access and independent evaluation.
